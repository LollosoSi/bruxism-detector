🚀 Quick Start Guide

Follow these steps to build your Bruxism Detector and set up the app. No coding required!

1. Gathering the Pieces

  • Any Windows, Linux or Mac computer to upload and occasionally update the program of the device.
  • (much recommended) A recent Android smartphone
  • 1x Arduino Uno R4 WiFi
  • 1x Olimex EMG shield
  • 1x EMG cable
  • At least 3 electrodes (reference here, can buy anywhere. Might have to replace regularly)
  • 1x Passive Buzzer (12mm).
  • 1x Push Button (12x12mm).
  • Male-female jumper wires.
  • Conductive gel (or ultrasound gel)
  • Elastic band to hold the electrodes. Like underwear ones.
  • Some iron wire to tie components to the 3D printed parts

2. 3D Printed Parts

All models can be found here (files or remixes sections)
You must source these parts yourself: at home, through a friend or service.
Mandatory:
  • Everything in V2 folder (base, top, light cover back and side)
  • 1x Clip for elastic band.
    Important notes:
    1. Print this in a soft material (TPU) for comfort.
    2. Scale it to fit your elastic band before printing!
Optional parts
Prints for the battery module:
  • "Battery holder" (in battery module folder).
    NOTE: This module will fit a 76mm wide, 26mm thick and 72mm long power bank module
  • Optionally print the holder cap ("Battery cover" file)
  • 2x "hook male"
Prints for the wall mount:
  • Everything in Wall mount folder (Mount and Lock)
  • 2x "hook male"
Phone mount (only makes sense to pair this with the wall mount):
  • Pieces 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 from the original model on thingiverse
  • Camera mount base and wall lock extension

3. Assembly

  • Remember that you're handling ESD sensitive devices. Take precautions.
  • Fit the Arduino and EMG shield into the 3D printed enclosure.
  • Connect the buzzer's positive lead to Pin 5 (negative to GND), and the button to Pin 3 (other end to GND).
  • The model you printed has some holes for iron wire, tie button and buzzer in place with two pieces.
Picture of the finished base

4. Electrode placement and headband

  • Place the ground electrode (black) on the center of your forehead, away from the temples.
  • Place the two signal electrodes (red and white) symmetrically near your temples, exactly where you feel the temporalis muscle contracting when you lightly clench your jaw.
  • Correct usage of the electrodes:
    • Move hair out of the way.
    • Use conductive gel or replacement pads — dry pads won't work reliably.
    • Avoid gluing your hair.
  • When you've found the correct spots, make corresponding holes into the elastic band, then clip electrodes through it.
Picture of the finished headband

Final steps

The next steps are simplified so anyone can complete the setup.
The catch is you'll need a recent Android phone with the companion app installed.
If you don't own one, you're going to use the desktop utility and need to edit the original program. Follow the steps for advanced users using the Arduino IDE. (link: WIP, refer to the old README)

4. Uploading the Software (Windows example)

Download the pre-compiled binary (main.bin) from the GitHub releases page.

  • Download arduino-cli and extract it in the same folder as main.bin.
  • Connect your Arduino via USB and open the command line in that folder.
    Find your COM port by running:
    arduino-cli.exe board list.
  • Upload the program by replacing COM6 with your actual port in this command:
    arduino-cli.exe upload -p COM6 -b arduino:renesas_uno:unor4wifi --input-file main.bin.

5. WiFi Setup

If you never launched the app yet, open it and allow all permissions it asks, all the time.
  • Turn on Bluetooth on your phone, open the app, and turn on the device. Enter your WiFi credentials when the dialog appears.
  • If the connection is successful, a "TCP" switch will show up. Tap "Set WiFi" and once again set the credentials of the network you're going to connect to.
  • Turn the TCP switch off, you only need this when the device needs to be connected to a new network or you're away from home.

6. SVM Training and Tuning

Wear the headband.
  • Step 1 - SVM Training:
    • Turn on the device and let it connect to WiFi. Long-press the button (within the first 2 minutes of runtime) until you hear a beep sequence.
    • The app will detect the device streaming data and enter the tuning screen automagically.
    • Hold the "Non-clenching" button to record data in the non clenching state: do not clench, stay still, move, cough, swallow, etc.
    • Hold the "Clenching" button to record data in the clenching state: begin the recording when you are already clenching. Don't go too hard, but do not let go. Add some variability. You may hold and release as many times as you like and the recordings will be merged.
    • Tap "Calculate weights"
    • You will see the results and hear beeping from the Arduino device, confirming the new settings have been saved automagically.
    • The device will restart.
  • Step 2 - SVM Tuning:
    • Turn on the device and let it connect to WiFi. Long-press the button (within the first 2 minutes of runtime) until you hear a beep sequence.
    • The app will detect the device streaming data and enter the tuning screen automagically.
    • Adjust the classification threshold just so it triggers during real clenches only.
    • Tap Awesome.
